Spain · Menorca · Two farms

An island of farmers
no one has heard of.

Menorca is the quietest of the Balearics, a biosphere reserve where the land is mostly worked by hand. The two homes are working farm estates — one built around a 14th-century watchtower with its own chapel and a private path to a cove, the other a 19th-century hilltop finca on 60 hectares with eleven bedrooms. Both make the same offer: the rarest currency in the Mediterranean — privacy, all of it yours.

A "silent guardian" 14th-century watchtower with an 18th-century farmhouse grown around it, a consecrated 1789 chapel, and 108 hectares of protected Menorcan countryside. Vaulted ceilings, original shutters, antiques chosen slowly by the family. A private path through olives and pine to Cala Mitjana, one of the island's least-photographed coves — 2.5 km of nobody but you.

Es Bec d'Aguila

A 19th-century hilltop finca, eleven bedrooms, 60 hectares — and a farmer named Xisco.

The Eagle's Beak — built in the 19th century by a family of Menorcan merchants to hunt, entertain and disappear. 1,000 square metres across three levels: a French garden with a fountain on the ground floor, a green-marble bar and library on the first, eleven en-suite bedrooms above. A 25-metre pool, a gym, a yoga studio overlooking the olives, a cinema room, a ceramic workshop. Farmer Xisco brings the morning vegetables, eggs and honey. The chef cooks Mediterranean. The concierge does the rest.
